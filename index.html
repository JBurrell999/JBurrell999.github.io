James Burrell

I'm a master's student at Columbia studying artificial intelligence and computer science, and a machine learning engineering intern at RTX. I work on the systems side of ML: efficient inference, quantization, GPU kernels, and getting models to give the same answer twice. Lately I've also been building with vision-language models for robot learning.

My current research asks whether static INT8 quantization can serve as a reproducibility primitive for transformer inference. Outside of research I like ML systems competitions like GPU MODE and AdaptFM, and hackathons. Most recently I built GetUp, which turns a phone video into a humanoid robot's motion, in one day at the Gemini 4 NYC hackathon.

I split my time between , & . You can find more of my work on GitHub and Devpost, and I'm always happy to talk about research or collaborations over email.

              GetUp
              Gemini · MuJoCo · 2026
            
            
              Phone video to humanoid robot. Gemini rebuilds the scene from any short clip, plans the motion, and judges
              side-by-side renders in a loop until a Unitree humanoid in MuJoCo matches the person. No mocap, no markers,
              about two minutes per video. Built in one day at the Gemini 4 NYC hackathon.

              IntChat
              research · INT8 · 2026
            
            
              Static W8A8 quantization as a reproducibility primitive for transformer inference, built on nanochat. Integer
              matmul makes logits exactly batch-invariant on one GPU, but at a fidelity cost, and it makes cross-platform
              determinism worse rather than better.
